01The brief: UniRide

IUB runs shuttle buses between pick-up points in Dhaka and the Bashundhara campus, all on paper today. UniRide is a web and Android system that digitises the whole service.

Route and trip management

Routes, stops, timetables; a bus and a driver assigned to each trip.

Seat reservation

Search, reserve 1+ seats, QR boarding pass, cancel before cut-off, automatic waitlist promotion.

Live tracking

GPS on every bus; the app shows the bus and the ETA at your stop.

Boarding and attendance

Driver scans the QR; no-shows flagged when the bus leaves.

Payment

Semester pass or per-trip wallet topped up via mobile financial service; every transaction auditable.

Notification and reporting

Delays, cancellations, waitlist, reminders; occupancy, on-time, revenue, no-show, fuel and maintenance reports.

03Task 1: product characterisation, model selection, lifecycle 10 marks

  • 1.1 Place UniRide in application domains (one sentence each).
  • 1.1 Four software vs hardware characteristics, each with a UniRide consequence.
  • 1.1 Three plausible failure causes, each mapped to the phase where the fault enters.
  • 1.1 Predict the maintenance split (corrective, adaptive, perfective, preventive) and justify from the brief.
  • 1.1 Two myths the Transport Office or your team may hold, with the reality.
  • 1.2 Shortlist three models; fill the criteria table; pick one in ≤ 150 words; say why each other one was rejected.
  • 1.3 Draw your 14-week lifecycle: time-boxes, milestones, artefacts, reviews, and where Tasks 2 to 8 happen.
How to approach it

Use the facts in the brief as evidence. Unsettled fee rules and one meeting a week point away from pure waterfall. A fixed 14-week window points to timeboxing. GPS, payment and peak load are risks a model must handle. Compare your choice using the model comparison table and the "choose the model" classifier in Lecture 2. For maintenance, start from the 20/48/16/16 baseline in Lecture 1 and adjust it (unsettled fee policy and new OS versions push adaptive up).

07Task 5: quality attributes 10 marks

  • 5.1 Rewrite the six stakeholder wishes in the six-part form, with metric, target, verification and ISO 25010 characteristic.
  • 5.2 Monthly scheduled service time in minutes (06:00 to 22:00, 30 days).
  • 5.2 Maximum downtime and error budget at 99.5%.
  • 5.2 Achieved availability from MTBF and MTTR; how many nines.
  • 5.2 % of error budget consumed by the two outages; the release policy that follows.
  • 5.3 Trade-off matrix for the six attributes; the two sharpest conflicts, decision, sacrifice and acceptance criterion.
How to approach it

The window is 16 hours a day, not 24: scheduled minutes = 16 × 60 × 30. Then budget = (1 − 0.995) × that. Check your arithmetic with the availability calculator in Lecture 5 (set the period to your scheduled hours). Use the six-part scenario and the trade-off matrix in the same lecture.

08Task 6: testing strategy and test design 18 marks

  • 6.1 V-model with test levels; unit, component, integration point, SUT, acceptance; environment and test data (GPS traces, payment responses).
  • 6.1 Integration strategy justified by your Task 3.1 modules; every stub and driver for payment and GPS.
  • 6.1 System-test entry and exit criteria; smoke-test content; regression selection policy.
  • 6.2 Flow graph of computeFare with nodes, edges, regions and predicate nodes.
  • 6.2 V(G) three ways; basis paths; one test per path with hand-computed expected output.
  • 6.2 Statement and branch coverage of your set; any untested branch and the extra test.
  • 6.3 Equivalence classes for every input of the reservation rule; boundary values (on, off, out).
  • 6.3 Decision table for conditions (i) to (v), collapsed with don't-cares; minimal test set with justification.
  • 6.4 Coverage, branch and mutation targets by module risk; CI/CD pipeline with tools; defect metrics with release gates.
How to approach it

Number the nodes from the given line numbers and count edges carefully: the V(G) calculator in Lecture 5 tells you if your three methods disagree. Watch out: computeFare has an early "return REJECT", which adds an edge to the exit. Use the BVA generator for 1 to 168 hours and 1 to 4 seats, and the UniCafe case as the pattern for the plan, gates and pipeline.
